The main friction is that the app looks forgiving, but several paths break its promise that someone can start with rough numbers and fill in details later. Preserve the current Clarity design and repair these interactions first. This review made no application changes.

Design specificity and strengths: Clarity's calm presentation, financial stage, savings milestone, and next-action list suit a person under financial pressure. No account or bank connection is required. Two-step setup, three fields per step, permission to skip, and secondary tools tucked away are sound foundations. Detailed spending/debt forms lose that guided quality.

## Priority issues

1. **[P1] The example number can produce a wrong-looking plan.** The income placeholder suggests `3,200`, but the calculation accepts `3200` and treats the comma version as unknown. Browser testing showed `3,200` with $2,500 costs and $150 debt minimums producing income Unknown and About -$2,650; `3200` correctly produced $550. No field error explains the difference. Accept ordinary currency formatting and distinguish invalid input from deliberate blanks. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`. Sources: index.html:86; logic.js:14; app.js:230,293.

2. **[P1] Skipping costs can make all income look available.** With only $3,200 income entered, the live dashboard showed About $3,200 in green as monthly breathing room. An empty expense side cannot support that conclusion. All-blank setup also shows several $0 amounts and a savings milestone. A separate calculation check with income $3,200, checking $450, savings $1,500, and no costs/debts assigned Investing & Wealth Building. Keep unknowns visible, withhold conclusions that depend on them, and offer one direct action such as Add an expense estimate. Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`. Sources: app.js:286; logic.js:158,175.

3. **[P1] Setup can discard typed numbers while the header says saved.** In a live phone-sized test, entering $450 checking, navigating Home, then returning to setup left the field blank. Setup saves on Next/Submit; other forms save while typing. Skip also does not capture currently typed setup fields. Preserve setup drafts as the user types, show accurate save feedback, and resume the unfinished step. Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`. Sources: app.js:110,132,228,239,245.

4. **[P1] Adding one bill replaces the whole rough total.** Live reproduction: $3,200 income minus $2,500 essential costs and $150 debt payments leaves $550. Adding only $1,400 rent changes the result to $1,650 because all other estimated costs disappear. The replacement notice does not support gradual entry. A direct logic check confirmed the same pattern for debt minimums: a $300 rough total became $50 after one card was entered. Retain the rough total while collecting detail, show both totals, and explicitly switch when the user confirms the list is complete. Suggested command: `$impeccable onboard`. Sources: logic.js:69,163; app.js:323.

5. **[P1] The next steps do not take the user to the next action.** Confirm every required bill and Set the weekly spending limit are plain text. The plan sends the user to Current Situation, which is not a navigation label; the actual destination is Cash & upcoming dates under More tools. After setup, the user must find and interpret more forms before getting a paycheck or weekly plan. Make next steps open their relevant fields and add actions inside empty plan sections, such as Add my next paycheck. Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`. Sources: app.js:306,354,356; logic.js:271; index.html:29.

## Additional first-use friction

- **Mental arithmetic before first value:** Reliable monthly income and Essential monthly costs still ask someone with variable pay to calculate two totals. Offer a small Help me estimate path, including weekly/two-week pay conversion and a few essential-cost prompts. Keep it optional.
- **Mobile arrival:** At 390 × 844 the introduction takes roughly 300 pixels below the header; Next and Skip are below the first screen. Shorten the introduction while retaining permission to use guesses. Labels and important hints include 11px text. Inputs themselves are 16px on mobile.
- **Detail overload:** Expense entry exposes seven input decisions. Debt entry exposes ten plus payoff strategy before any debt exists. Start with the few useful fields and tuck the rest under More details (optional). Explain Required / legally sensitive in ordinary language.
- **Correction friction:** Expense and debt rows offer Remove but no Edit or Undo.
- **Small inconsistencies:** The milestone can say $1,000 while its immediate progress target is $250; explain that $250 is the first step. A known zero overdue amount reopens blank. Debt summary can show $0 even while its empty-state text says no entered debts is different from zero.

## Cognitive load, emotional journey, and personas

Setup has low visible complexity: three fields at a time, useful grouping, and a clear primary action. It still requires users to recall or calculate financial totals. Detailed forms fail chunking, one decision at a time, minimal choices, and gradual disclosure. The expense category list has 37 choices within three groups; the debt form has ten decisions plus two strategies.

The emotional journey begins with reassurance, then weakens at the moment a plan is supposed to feel useful: unclear results and generic instructions turn relief into uncertainty. End setup with one credible conclusion and a direct next action.

For a first-time user, the comma example and nonexistent Current Situation label are concrete traps. For a distracted phone user, draft loss and a tall introduction interrupt progress. For a keyboard or screen-reader user, semantic labels and focus support are strengths; action rows cannot be activated, and duplicated numbering appears in the accessibility tree (spoken output was not independently verified).
